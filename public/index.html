The aircraft

Three bodies. One axle. The shell does the flying.

A cabin that hangs level, two wings that stay level, and an egg-shaped shell that rotates a quarter turn around both. The fan and the battery live in the shell. On the pad the shell stands up and blows down. In cruise it lies on its side and blows back, and the wings carry the weight.

Why an egg

One fan does both jobs

The shell is the nacelle of a tilting fan. No separate lift rotors, no open blades, no second propulsion system to carry around in cruise.

Why it stays level

The cabin hangs from the axle

Tail-sitters of the 1950s laid the pilot on his back to take off. Juddster rotates the shell around the people instead of the people around the shell.

Safety in layers

Two motors, two batteries, one parachute

Thirty percent spare thrust. Either fan stage flies alone to a pad. If both fail, a whole-aircraft parachute brings it down base first.

Configurations

Three ways to meet the ground.

Spread legs anywhere. Lying down for step-in boarding and low wind load. A landing cradle at each farm that holds the pod, charges it and meets the door with a platform.

Boarding

Two seats, a window to your knees

You and one companion, with a glass strip from the crown of the pod to the floor. The pod charges on the farm while you taste.

Flown automatically

Fixed, surveyed, repeated

No pilot on board. The route is flown the same way every time with an operator watching from the ground and a wind limit at every pad.

Wind

Designed for the Cape Doctor

Each pad reads the wind and the aircraft lands into it. On the ground it squats low on a wide stance, or lies down so the wind has little to push against.

Programme

From model to first passengers.

Three funded stages, each one proving the thing the next depends on. The first is the go or no-go point for the whole aircraft.

Stage 1

Airflow study and scale model

A one-fifth scale flying model proves the rotating transition and measures the drag of the shell, the largest unknown in the design.

Stage 2

Half-scale demonstrator

An unmanned 80 kg aircraft flies the full profile: lift-off, transition, cruise, and landing on its legs.

Stage 3

Full-scale prototype

A 650 kg single-seat pod flies unmanned for one hour, then the two-seat shuttle follows for certification.

Questions

Things people ask.

Has it flown?

Not yet. Juddster is a design in development with a provisional patent and a full engineering specification. The next step is a scale model. Registering interest helps us plan the first route and places no obligation on you.

Who flies it?

Nobody on board. The aircraft flies fixed, surveyed routes automatically with a ground operator watching. Approval for pilotless passenger flight is the longest part of the programme and the fare assumes it.

What happens if the fan stops?

The fan has two stages on two motors and two batteries, so a single failure still gives enough thrust to fly on the wings to a pad. If both fail, a parachute lowers the whole aircraft. The design also has a thirty percent margin of thrust in the hover.

Why does the cabin stay level?

Because the cabin hangs from an axle and the shell turns around it. The axle, the cabin and the wings form one level body, and the shell carries the fan and the battery around them.

How far can it go?

The two-seat shuttle flies 55 to 75 km with reserve, which covers the Stellenbosch and Franschhoek farms from the Waterfront. A single-seat version with a larger wing and battery is designed for one hour in the air.
