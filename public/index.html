Technology

Three bodies on one axle.

A cabin that hangs level, a pair of wings that stay level, and an egg-shaped shell that rotates a quarter turn around both. The fan and the battery live in the shell. Patent pending.

Why an egg

The shell is the nacelle of a tilting fan. In the hover it stands up and blows down. In cruise it lies on its side and blows back, and the wings carry the weight. One fan does both jobs.

Safety in layers

Thirty percent spare thrust. Two motors on two battery strings, so one failure still flies to a pad. Wings that brake like a dragster's chute. A parachute for the whole aircraft.

Three ways to land

Spread legs anywhere. Lying down on its belly for step-in boarding and low wind load. A landing cradle at each farm that holds the pod, charges it and meets the door with a platform.

Questions

Things people ask.

Has it flown?

Not yet. Juddster is a design in development with a provisional patent and a full engineering specification. The next step is a scale model. Registering interest helps us plan the first route and places no obligation on you.

Who flies it?

Nobody on board. The aircraft flies fixed, surveyed routes automatically with a ground operator watching. Approval for pilotless passenger flight is the longest part of the programme and the fare assumes it.

What happens if the fan stops?

The fan has two stages on two motors and two batteries, so a single failure still gives enough thrust to fly on the wings to a pad. If both fail, a parachute lowers the whole aircraft. The design also has a thirty percent margin of thrust in the hover.

What about the wind?

The Cape Doctor is designed in. Each pad has a wind sensor and a limit, the aircraft lands into the wind, and on the ground it stands on a wide stance or lies down so the wind has little to push against.

Why does the cabin stay level?

Because the cabin hangs from an axle and the shell turns around it. Tail-sitter aircraft of the 1950s laid their pilot on his back to take off. Juddster turns the shell instead of the person.

How far can it go?

The two-seat shuttle flies 55 to 75 km with reserve, which covers the Stellenbosch and Franschhoek farms from the Waterfront. A single-seat version with a larger wing and battery is designed for one hour in the air.
